# **Titanic Survival Analysis & Data Exploration**
## **Project Overview**
This project performs an Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) on the famous Titanic dataset using Python. The primary objective is to clean messy real-world data, analyze survival patterns across various demographic and socioeconomic factors, and visualize key findings to answer core business questions.

## **Key Questions Addressed**
1. **Gender Impact:** Did female passengers have a higher survival rate compared to male passengers?
2. **Socioeconomic Class:** How did ticket class (`Pclass`) influence survival probability?
3. **Age Groups:** What was the survival distribution across different age demographics (children, adults, seniors)?
4. **Family Structure:** How did traveling with family members affect individual survival chances?


## **Tech Stack & Tools**
- **Language:** Python
- **Environment:** Google Colab / Jupyter Notebook
- **Libraries:** 
  - `pandas` - Data loading, manipulation, and cleaning
  - `numpy` - Numerical operations
  - `matplotlib` & `seaborn` - Data visualization


## **Key Findings & Insights**
- **Gender Dynamics:** Female passengers survived at a rate of **74.20%**, compared to **18.89%** for male passengers, illustrating the strict enforcement of the "women and children first" protocol.
- **Class Disparity:** Passengers in **1st Class** had the highest survival rate (**62.96%**), whereas **3rd Class** passengers had the lowest (**24.24%**).
- **Age Distribution:** Children aged 0–12 had the highest survival probability (**57.97%**), while seniors aged 60+ had the lowest (**22.73%**).
- **Family Effect:** Passengers traveling with family members had a significantly higher survival rate (**50.57%**) compared to those traveling alone (**30.35%**).
